Producer → NIYA → Canadian buyer The basic model

The basic model is simple: producer, NIYA, buyer.

We are not buying inexpensive goods overseas to resell at a markup. We represent selected producers and work to build their business in Canada — learning each company well enough to speak for it commercially.

Stage one

The producer

A small or emerging business — a coffee farm, food maker, textile workshop — with a quality product but no route to international customers.

→
Stage two

NIYA Global Partners

We evaluate the product, pricing, capacity and reliability, then act as the producer's Canadian-facing commercial representative.

→
Stage three

The Canadian buyer

A retailer, roaster, distributor or importer who gets a credible, pre-evaluated opportunity — not a random product pitch.

We create value on both sides of the relationship.

NIYA only works if the producer gains real access to customers, and the Canadian buyer gets a product that makes commercial sense on its own terms.

For the producer

Keep producing. Let us build the Canadian market.

  • No need to hire a Canadian sales team or learn the market alone
  • A representative who understands your product and speaks for it in Canada
  • Introductions, samples, and negotiations handled on your behalf
  • You succeed when NIYA succeeds — the incentives are aligned
For the Canadian buyer

Opportunities that have already been vetted.

  • Quality, pricing, capacity and reliability evaluated before you hear about it
  • Clear commercial information: minimum orders, shipping, compliance, margin
  • One point of contact instead of navigating an unfamiliar supplier directly
  • Over time, a track record you can trust when NIYA brings the next opportunity
"Our first objective is not to become a million-dollar company. Our first objective is to complete one legitimate transaction in which an international producer gains a Canadian customer because of NIYA."
That transaction proves the model. Then we repeat it.

Starting narrow, on purpose.

We are not approaching hundreds of companies across dozens of countries. Ethiopian coffee and a small set of export-ready products are where we learn the model deeply before expanding it.

01

Identify producers

Find promising Ethiopian producers with export potential and real production capacity.

02

Evaluate rigorously

Assess quality, pricing, documentation and reliability before agreeing to represent anyone.

03

Validate demand

Test genuine interest among Canadian buyers before committing money to inventory.

04

Prove, then repeat

Close one legitimate transaction, learn from it, and build the next relationship.

A small team, where every contribution matters.

At this stage, NIYA is deliberately lean. Two roles carry most of the day-to-day weight.

Shahin

Customer relations & business development

Central to NIYA's communication with both sides — introducing NIYA professionally, understanding buyer needs, following up, and turning introductions into real commercial opportunities. This is relationship building, not customer service: people need to trust the person representing NIYA.

Raul

Financial management

Protects the financial health of every transaction — pricing, commissions, currency conversion, freight, duties, and cash flow. A transaction can generate large revenue and still be a bad deal if the costs are misunderstood, so financial discipline starts here.
